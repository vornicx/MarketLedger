**Morning Edition — 24 September 2026 · ~09:10 CEST snapshot**

## The bond selloff breaks the relief trade — oil diplomacy is no longer enough

The cross-asset regime has turned defensive. Strong US business activity, a weak Treasury auction and hawkish Fed guidance pushed the US 10-year yield above 5%, while Wednesday's oil rebound revived inflation risk. Brent is easing again this morning and US-China trade talks offer a stabiliser, but equities, crypto and gold are now trading against a materially tighter rates backdrop. **Regime: mixed / defensive risk-off, led by rates.**

## Daily Brief

1. **What changed:** the rates ceiling broke. US 10Y reached 5.106% on Wednesday and was around 5.125% Thursday morning after the flash US Composite PMI rose to 58.4, its highest since July 2021, and a weak $70bn five-year Treasury auction amplified selling.
2. **How markets reacted:** the S&P 500 fell 0.75%, Nasdaq 1.13% and Dow 0.68% on Wednesday; Asia ex-Japan fell 0.94% Thursday and US/European equity futures pointed lower. BTC fell more than 2% to about $83,900.
3. **What could change the picture:** a sustained retreat in US 10Y back below 5%, softer Fed repricing and durable oil de-escalation would reopen the relief trade; renewed Brent above $105 or yields extending higher would deepen risk-off.
4. Brent eased about 0.9% to $102.16 after Wednesday's near-4% rebound; WTI was about $91.39. Iran says diplomacy should continue, but the sides remain far apart.
5. DXY held near a two-month high around 101.08; October Fed-hike odds rose to roughly 70% after strong activity data and hawkish guidance.
6. Japan's 10-year government bond yield jumped 10 bp to 3.075%, the highest since August 1996, showing the bond stress is global rather than purely US-specific.
7. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US and China had reached a deal to extend their trade truce; Trump-Xi remains the main geopolitical upside catalyst today.

## Regime

**Mixed / defensive risk-off, led by rates.** Oil is off Thursday's early highs and diplomacy remains alive, but the dominant transmission chain is now **strong growth + oil/inflation risk + weak bond demand → higher yields → stronger dollar → pressure on duration, equities and crypto**. The relief trade is not restored while US 10Y remains above 5%.

## World & Macro

US flash Composite PMI rose to **58.4**, the strongest since July 2021. The growth signal is positive in isolation, but markets interpreted it through inflation and policy: strong demand strained supply chains and increased price pressure, strengthening the case for more Fed tightening.

US-China relations provide the main counterweight. Bessent said Washington and Beijing agreed to extend the trade truce, while Trump and Xi meet with trade, technology, Taiwan and Iran in focus. On Iran, talks continue but the gap remains large.

## Rates & Bonds

US 2Y rose to **4.891%** on Wednesday after touching 4.947%; US 10Y closed around **5.106%** and traded near **5.125%** Thursday morning, the highest area since 2007. A weak $70bn five-year auction cleared at the highest auction yield since 2007 and helped accelerate the selloff.

Japan's 10Y JGB rose **10 bp to 3.075%**, the highest since August 1996. Fed Governor Michael Barr said further hikes will likely be needed; markets price roughly a **70%** probability of another October increase.

**Interpretation:** this is no longer a benign steepening. The hurdle rate for risk assets has risen materially.

## Energy & Commodities

Brent fell about **0.9% to $102.16** and WTI **0.8% to $91.39** Thursday morning after Wednesday's near-4% oil rebound. Iran says diplomacy must continue, but Hormuz remains conditional and military options remain part of US rhetoric. US crude inventories rose **3.0m barrels to 426.4m**, versus expectations for a 641k draw.

Gold was little changed around **$4,291.48/oz** after Wednesday's 1.64% fall. Higher yields and the stronger dollar are offsetting safe-haven demand. Copper supply risk deserves monitoring after BHP suspended operations at Escondida following a worker death; no price claim is made here without a reliable live print.

## United States

Wednesday: **S&P 500 -0.75%, Nasdaq -1.13%, Dow -0.68%**. Nine of 11 S&P sectors fell; declining stocks outnumbered advancers by 1.9-to-1. SOX fell 1.2%, Nvidia 1.5%, Alphabet 3.8% and Amazon 2.2%. Thursday S&P e-minis were down about **0.23%** in the Asian snapshot.

## Europe

The STOXX 600 fell **0.44%** Wednesday. Thursday futures pointed lower: Euro Stoxx 50 about **-0.35%**, DAX **-0.37%**, FTSE **-0.34%**. Euro-zone activity has been more resilient than expected, but the combination of energy sensitivity and global bond repricing is a near-term constraint. ECB officials continue to emphasise meeting-by-meeting decisions.

## Asia

MSCI Asia ex-Japan fell **0.94%**. Japan's Nikkei rose **1.30%** after reopening from a three-day holiday, while Australia fell 0.7% and China's CSI300 fell 1.29%. The split reflects Japan's catch-up in equities against broader concern about yields and trade/geopolitics.

## FX

DXY held near **101.08**, a two-month high. EUR/USD was around **1.1383–1.1384**, GBP/USD around **1.3240**, and USD/JPY around **157.8–157.9** after Japan repeated that the principles behind July's coordinated intervention remain intact. A move toward 160 still carries intervention asymmetry.

## Crypto

BTC traded near **$83,900**, down more than 2% over 24 hours after touching nearly $87,300. ETH and SOL were down roughly 2%–3%; XRP fell about 5% and DOGE about 7%. The immediate macro driver is higher Treasury yields rather than a crypto-specific shock.

The prior institutional-flow support remains relevant, but the durability test is now harder: BTC is below $85k and Friday brings an approximately **$14bn options expiry**. Recent strong ETF/ETP demand is historical confirmation, not a guarantee against a rates-led pullback.
