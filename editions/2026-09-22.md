## AI breadth extends the risk rally — but higher-for-longer rates are rebuilding the ceiling

The relief trade strengthened overnight: the Nasdaq closed at a record, Asian technology followed and Bitcoin pushed to an eight-month high above $87,000 before easing. The tension is that the dollar remains firm, Fed hike expectations have risen again and leverage is rebuilding quickly in crypto. **Regime: mixed / constructive risk-on, with hawkish-rate and geopolitical confirmation still required.**

## Daily Brief

1. **What changed:** Monday's relief became a confirmed US cash-session move. The S&P 500 gained 1.49%, the Nasdaq 2.26% to a record close and the PHLX semiconductor index 4.3%; AMD reached a $1 trillion market value.
2. **How markets reacted:** Asian technology extended the move on Tuesday: MSCI Asia-Pacific rose about 0.75%, Taiwan reached records and South Korea was up as much as 2%. BTC traded above $87,000 overnight before easing roughly 2%; Brent stabilised around $100–101 after Monday's sharp fall.
3. **What could change the picture:** the Fed remains hawkish. Market pricing for another October hike rose to about 56% from 43.5%, while DXY held around 100.46 and USD/JPY near 157.4. A renewed oil shock or another rise in yields would challenge the rally.
4. Oil is no longer falling in a straight line: Brent recovered toward $100.8 as traders waited for possible US-Iran talks. The inflation-relief channel remains helpful, but geopolitics can reverse it quickly.
5. Gold fell about 0.6% to $4,319/oz as higher-for-longer rates and a firm dollar outweighed safe-haven demand.
6. Crypto breadth is strong, but derivatives are hotter: CoinGlass data cited by CoinDesk showed $647.9m of shorts among $746.6m of 24-hour liquidations, while open interest rose 7.59% to $156bn.
7. Japan remains a currency risk point: the BoJ raised its policy rate to 1.25% last week, yet the yen weakened toward 157.5 per dollar and intervention risk remains live.

## Regime

**Mixed / constructive risk-on.** Observable confirmation comes from a record Nasdaq, strong semiconductor breadth, positive Asian follow-through and a lower 10-year Treasury yield around 4.95% versus above 5% last week. The counterweight is a firm dollar, renewed Fed-hike pricing, still-expensive energy near $100 Brent and rebuilding crypto leverage.

## World & Macro

The dominant causal chain remains **Middle East diplomacy → oil → inflation expectations → bond yields → duration assets**. Monday's oil fall removed some inflation pressure and allowed technology and crypto to reprice higher. Tuesday's stabilisation in crude means the easy part of that relief may already be behind us.

The next geopolitical fork is unusually concentrated: potential US-Iran discussions around the UN General Assembly and the Trump-Xi meeting expected on 24 September. The latter can affect trade, semiconductors, AI policy and Asian FX simultaneously.

## Rates & Bonds

The Fed raised the funds range to 3.75%–4.00% last week and remains biased toward further tightening. Reuters reported the implied probability of another October hike rising to roughly 56%. The US 10-year yield eased to about 4.95% on Monday, below the 5% threshold that had pressured valuations.

**Interpretation:** lower long yields are currently helping growth assets, but the front end and dollar still reflect a restrictive policy path. Today’s Fed speakers and the US 2-year Treasury auction are the cleanest near-term tests of that tension.

## Energy & Commodities

Brent fell below $100 on Monday before recovering to roughly $100.8 early Tuesday; WTI was around $92.8. The move remains primarily geopolitical/supply-driven rather than evidence of a sudden global demand collapse. Gold fell 0.6% to about $4,319.39/oz; silver fell 1.1% to $65.28 as the stronger-dollar/higher-rate channel dominated.

## Crypto

That combination says leverage was replaced quickly rather than washed out.

ETH and SOL participated strongly; Monday snapshots put ETH around the high-$2,700s and SOL around the high-$110s. The next confirmation must come from sustained spot demand and ETF flows rather than perpetual leverage alone. Friday US spot Bitcoin ETF inflows were reported around **$433m** by JPMorgan via WSJ; that is supportive but dated, so it is not treated as today's flow.
