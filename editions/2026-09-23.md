**Morning Edition — 23 September 2026 · ~09:00 CEST snapshot**

## Oil relief extends the AI risk bid — but the dollar and front-end rates still set the ceiling

Oil has fallen for a sixth session as Saudi supply routes improve and diplomacy around Iran gains attention. Asian technology is extending its rally, Bitcoin is holding near $87,000 and long yields remain below the 5% stress line. The offset is a two-month high in the dollar and a US 2-year yield at its highest since mid-2024. **Regime: mixed / constructive risk-on, with a hawkish rates ceiling.**

## Daily Brief

1. **What changed:** Brent fell below $99 as Saudi Arabia restarted the East-West pipeline and markets assigned more weight to possible US-Iran diplomacy.
2. **How markets reacted:** Asia ex-Japan rose 0.3% for a sixth straight gain, Taiwan added 0.7% near records and South Korea gained 0.5%; BTC held near $86,900 while US and European equity futures were slightly positive.
3. **What could change the picture:** the rates ceiling is firmer. US 2Y reached 4.7879%, futures imply a 54% chance of another Fed hike in October and DXY rose to 100.71, a two-month high.
4. Brent was about $98.2–$98.5 and WTI about $89.0–$89.3, both at roughly two-week lows after six declines.
5. USD/JPY remained near 157.6 despite the BoJ hike; intervention asymmetry remains live as 160 approaches.
6. Gold eased about 0.6% to $4,329 while copper remained near records after gaining roughly 18% year-to-date.
7. Crypto received stronger institutional confirmation from Monday's US spot ETF flows: Bitcoin ETFs took in $998.95m and Ether ETFs $269.98m, the strongest daily inflows in about 11 months for BTC funds.

## Regime

**Mixed / constructive risk-on.** Falling oil, contained long yields, broad Asian AI participation and strong crypto spot-ETF demand support risk assets. The regime is not clean risk-on because the dollar is at a two-month high, US 2Y is at its highest since mid-2024 and Fed speakers continue to validate a restrictive path.

## World & Macro

The main transmission chain is now **improving Gulf supply + diplomacy → lower oil → less near-term inflation pressure → calmer long yields → support for duration and crypto**. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline restart improves the ability to move crude to the Red Sea while bypassing Hormuz. At the same time, diplomacy remains fragile: constructive comments coexist with explicit military threats.

Chinese President Xi Jinping is due in Washington ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting, adding a second macro fork around trade, AI and semiconductors.

## Rates & Bonds

Treasury futures strengthened as oil fell, keeping the US 10-year yield below 5%. The front end is less friendly: the US 2-year yield reached **4.7879%**, its highest since mid-2024. Fed funds futures imply about a **54% probability of another October hike** and roughly **33 bp of tightening by year-end**.

**Interpretation:** falling oil is buying relief at the long end, but the front end says the market still expects restrictive policy. That divergence is the key rates signal today.

## Energy & Commodities

Brent traded around **$98.16–$98.46**, down roughly 1%, while WTI was around **$89.01–$89.32**, down 1.3%–1.7%. Both have fallen for six consecutive sessions. The driver is primarily improved supply access and diplomacy, not a clear collapse in demand.

Gold fell about **0.6% to $4,329.31/oz** as higher-for-longer rates and a stronger dollar outweighed safe-haven demand. Copper remained near record territory after rising about **18% year-to-date**.

## United States

Tuesday's Nasdaq reached a fresh intraday record at **27,212.68** as technology and semiconductors retained leadership. At Wednesday's Asian snapshot, S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures were fractionally positive. The constructive element is persistent AI breadth; the constraint is the front-end rate move and stronger dollar.

## Europe

EURO STOXX 50, DAX and FTSE futures were all up roughly **0.4%** in the early snapshot. Lower crude helps European margins and inflation mechanically, but Europe still faces energy sensitivity into winter and potential diesel-supply complications. No unsupported cash-index print is manufactured before the session develops.

## Asia

MSCI Asia-Pacific ex-Japan rose **0.3%**, extending gains to a sixth session. South Korea gained **0.5%**, Samsung rose more than 2% and Taiwan added **0.7%** near all-time highs. Chinese blue chips slipped **0.5%**. Japan was closed for a holiday; Nikkei futures traded at 66,735, well above Friday's cash close.

## FX

DXY rose **0.16% to 100.71**, its strongest level in two months. EUR/USD was near **1.1428**, GBP/USD **1.3316** and USD/JPY around **157.58–157.60**. The dollar is still being paid for rate differentials even as falling oil improves the inflation outlook.

## Crypto

Bitcoin held near **$86,900**, up about 1% over 24 hours in the Asian snapshot. XRP gained about 6%, while ETH and SOL were up less than 1%. The stronger confirmation is institutional: US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded **$998.95m** of net inflows on Monday, their largest daily inflow in 11 months, while spot Ether ETFs took in **$269.98m**.

That does not remove leverage risk from the prior breakout, but it improves the composition of the move because regulated spot vehicles are showing material demand. The next test is whether BTC can hold the $85k area without a renewed acceleration in funding/open interest.
